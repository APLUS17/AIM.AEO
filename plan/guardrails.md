# Guardrails for the blog workflow

Source: the Perplexity write-up "How to master content for consulting B2B service" (uploaded Oct 18, 2026). **Proposed, not approved.** Nothing here changes the live pattern until you say so.

Checked before using it: Google's generative AI guide (updated July 10, 2026) says SEO best practices remain relevant and that AEO and GEO are "still SEO". Bing's AI Performance report (public preview, Feb 10, 2026) shows citations, cited pages and sampled grounding queries, and says citation counts do not show ranking or placement. The funnel framework itself comes from vendor blogs and LinkedIn posts, and the write-up labels its cadence, offers and 90-day plan as recommendations, not benchmarks. We treat them the same way.

## The rules

1. **The blog is a system, not a schedule.** Every post answers one buyer question and ends on one next step. Stage is set by the question, not the format.
2. **Define buyer and first purchase before writing more.** One buyer segment. One first purchase (the write-up suggests a fixed-scope diagnostic after a free self-assessment, which for us is the grader).
3. **Never promise outcomes.** No guaranteed mentions, no secret formatting. Sell a process and defined deliverables. (Already in the pattern.)
4. **No client results means we say so.** Label own tests and independent analyses. Separate proposed fixes from measured results. Never imply a business was a client. (Already in the source policy; P03 follows it.)
5. **Two kinds of page.** Educational posts answer questions. Commercial pages explain scope, evidence, fit and next step. The blog does the first; the site needs the second.
6. **Five recurring themes** keep the feed from turning into AI news: buyer problems, education, method, proof, buying decisions.
7. **One substantial piece, then adapt it.** Each article gets adapted per channel (different job each), not pasted. Reduce volume if it hurts substance.
8. **Channels earn their place.** Website, one social channel and email first. Facebook needs a specific reason to exist.
9. **Two scorecards.** Business: qualified inquiries by source, inquiry-to-meeting, meetings to opportunities, wins. Visibility: cited, mentioned, referred, converted, kept separate.
10. **Manual tests record engine, date, question set, method and limits.** P03 does this.

## What I would change, in order

1. **Define the offer** (Yellow, one sitting): who buys first, what the diagnostic contains and costs to describe, where "book" goes. Until then the 10 G+B calls to action point at nothing.
2. **Build three commercial pages**: service page, sample diagnostic (we can run it on a public site and label it independent), "how we measure". These are site pages, not blog posts.
3. **Pick the order of industries** instead of ten in a row. The write-up warns against "B2B companies" as a segment; ten verticals is the same problem.
4. **Add a business scorecard**: tagged links on every call to action and a "how did you hear about us" field on the grader. Free, no keys.
5. **Consider cutting to 3 posts a week** and using the freed slot for adapted social and email. Your call.
