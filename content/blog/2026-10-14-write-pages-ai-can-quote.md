An AI answer is assembled from passages. If the answer to a buyer's question is buried under three paragraphs of warm-up, the engine has little to work with. It may quote a competitor's clearer page instead, or fill the gap with a guess.

These five checks work on any page: pricing, product, about, FAQ. Each takes a few minutes.

## The five checks

### 1. Put the answer first

Open every section with the answer, then explain. If a heading asks "Who is it for?", the first sentence under it should say who it is for. Detail, caveats and background come after.

### 2. Use headings that match real questions

"How much does it cost?" beats "Pricing overview". Buyers ask AI full questions, and a heading written like the question makes the matching passage easy to find. Use the words your customers use, not your internal names.

### 3. Be specific

Replace adjectives with facts: the price, the plan limits, who it suits, what is included, what is not. Only state what you can support. A vague claim gives an engine nothing to quote. A wrong claim is worse.

| Before | After (example) |
|---|---|
| "We offer flexible plans for growing teams." | "Plans start at $99 a month, with a free trial." |
| "Trusted by leading brands." | "Used by [number] agencies with 10 to 50 people." (fill in a number you can verify) |

### 4. One claim per sentence, in plain words

Short sentences are easier to quote accurately. Name the subject. A sentence that begins "It also does this" loses its meaning the moment it is lifted out of the page, so write "AiM also tracks your mentions every week" instead.

### 5. Show when it was updated and who stands behind it

Add a visible "last updated" date to pages with prices, features or policies, and make clear which company the page speaks for. People use that to judge freshness. Machines can too.

## A two-minute test

Copy only the first screen of a page into ChatGPT or Claude. Ask: "What does this company sell, who is it for, and what does it cost?"

> If the answer is wrong or vague, the page is wrong or vague.
