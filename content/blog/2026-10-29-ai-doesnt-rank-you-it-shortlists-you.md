A search page hands the buyer ten links and lets them do the comparing. An AI answer does the comparing first and names a handful of options. Ranking did not stop mattering. It stopped being the finish line.

## What changed for the buyer

| | A search results page | An AI answer |
|---|---|---|
| What the buyer sees | A list of links to compare | A written answer that names a few options |
| What the buyer does next | Opens several pages | Often reads the answer and stops |
| Where you win | A high position | A place in the answer |

The last row is our summary. The middle row has evidence. Pew Research tracked the Google searches of 900 US adults in March 2025. When a search showed an AI summary, people clicked a regular result link in 8% of visits. When it did not, they clicked in 15%. They clicked a link inside the summary in 1% of visits. Pew also found 26% of visits with a summary ended the browsing session, against 16% without.

That is one panel, one country, and Google only. It shows a pattern, not what will happen to your traffic.

## An answer names a few, not one

The version of this idea that says AI "selects one answer" is too strong. When we asked a search engine for the best AEO agency for a small business, each written answer named about five firms. We describe that test in [We ran our own five-question test](/blog/we-ran-our-own-five-question-test.html). The honest summary is that AI gives the buyer a shortlist.

Google's guide to its AI features defines "query fan-out" as "a set of concurrent, related queries generated by the model." If that is how the answer gets built, being relevant to the related questions around a topic matters more than ranking for one phrase.

## Is SEO dead

No. Google's guide to generative AI features says "The best practices for SEO continue to be relevant", and says optimizing for generative AI search is still SEO. Treat ranking as one input to the shortlist, not the goal.

> Ranking gets you considered. The answer decides who gets named.

## Three things we think decide who is named

These are our reading, not measured results. We do not know the internal rules of any engine.

1. **Be clearly one thing.** If the engine can't tell who you are, it can't name you. In our own test, searches for AiM returned other companies with similar names. Use one name, one description, and the same business details on your site and your listings.
2. **Be named by others.** In our test, the lists for "best agency" questions were mostly written by the firms on them. Mentions on pages you don't control, such as directories, reviews, and press, are one way into those lists.
3. **Put the answer on the page, in the HTML.** Vercel found in December 2024 that OpenAI's and Anthropic's crawlers fetched JavaScript files but did not run them. Text that only appears after JavaScript runs can be missed. Put prices, services, and answers in plain page text. [Write pages AI can quote](/blog/write-pages-ai-can-quote.html) shows how.

## What to do this week

1. Ask three AI tools the question your buyer asks, and write down who is named.
2. If you are not on the list, check whether someone with a similar name is.
3. Fix the first screen of your main service page so it answers the question in two sentences.
