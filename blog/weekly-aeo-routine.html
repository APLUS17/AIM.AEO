A 60-minute weekly AEO routine for small teams

You don't need a dedicated team. One hour a week, in four blocks, keeps you ahead of drift in what AI says about you.

AEO is not a one-off project. Models update, search indexes refresh, and the pages AI reads about you change. An answer that names you today can drop you next month.

You don't need a team to keep up. You need the same hour, every week, run the same way.

The routine

BlockTimeWhat you do
1. Test20 minAsk the same five questions on ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity. Log the answers.
2. Compare10 minCheck against last week. Are you still mentioned? Did your position move? Any new errors or new competitors?
3. Fix20 minPick one thing and fix it.
4. Record10 minWrite down the date, what you changed, and what to check next week.

1. Test

Use the five-question set from Five questions to test your brand in ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity. Keep the wording identical every week, or you can't tell what moved. Use a fresh chat for each question and run each one more than once.

2. Compare

Keep the log in a simple spreadsheet. One row per question per engine, with columns for the date, whether you were mentioned, your position, what was said, anything wrong, and the competitors named. A change you can see in two consecutive weeks is worth acting on. A single odd answer is not.

3. Fix

Choose one fix only. When you change five things at once, you never learn which one worked. Good first picks:

  • A wrong fact: What to do when AI gets your brand wrong
  • A weak page: Write pages AI can quote
  • A competitor that keeps showing up in your place: read the page the engine cites for them and ask what it says that yours doesn't

4. Record

Note what you changed and the date. Next week's comparison depends on it.

Once a month

Re-run the crawl checks from Can AI crawlers read your site?. A firewall rule or a site redesign can block the bots without anyone noticing.

What to expect

Small, steady fixes beat one big push.

Don't expect overnight movement. Some engines search the live web and may reflect page changes sooner. Others change more slowly. Nobody can promise a date, which is exactly why a log matters: it shows you the trend even when a single week looks flat.
